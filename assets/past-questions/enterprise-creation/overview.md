# Enterprise Creation and Entrepreneurship Paper Overview

## Objective
The exam evaluates the candidate's core business development and economic insight to:
* Define and analyze the traits, benefits, and mindsets of a 21st-century entrepreneur.
* Navigate the comprehensive stages of the entrepreneurial process, from ideation to venture growth.
* Formulate, interpret, and defend a comprehensive Business Plan, highlighting its core structural components.
* Understand fundamental microeconomic and macroeconomic theories, including market supply/demand and national income metrics.
* Differentiate between corporate personality types and structural liabilities under business regulations.

---

## Paper Content
* **Paper Name:** Enterprise Creation and Entrepreneurship
* **Nature of Exam:** Written
* **Duration:** 2 Hours
* **Credit Value:** 1
* **Structure:** A cross-cutting national exam paper mandatory for all specialties. The exam is strictly split into 3 core sections: Section A (Entrepreneurship), Section B (General Economics), and Section C (Company Law).

---

## What You Should Know
To successfully clear this multi-disciplinary business paper, you must master:
* **Entrepreneurship Foundations:** Distinctions between entrepreneurship and intrapreneurship; classifications of entrepreneurs (innovators vs. imitators); and assessing external funding infrastructure within Cameroon (commercial bank loans, microfinance, venture funding).
* **Business Planning Mechanics:** Structuring a complete Business Plan; justifying why the Executive Summary functions as its most critical asset; and detailing the strategic importance of business modeling for tech-driven startups.
* **General Economic Principles:** Price theory, market competition, and graphical modeling of shifts in demand vs. changes in quantity demanded; supply-side metrics; structural difficulties in computing National Income; and core banking operations.
* **Company Law Principles:** Core fundamentals of the corporate personality principle; structural differences between Private Limited Companies (Ltd) and Public Limited Companies (Plc) under regional OHADA laws; and identifying shareholder contribution formats.
